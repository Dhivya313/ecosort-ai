# Paper Waste

## Examples
- Newspapers, magazines, and catalogs
- Office paper and printer paper
- Cardboard boxes (corrugated and paperboard)
- Paper bags
- Envelopes (including those with plastic windows)
- Junk mail and flyers
- Paper towel and toilet paper rolls (the cardboard tube)
- Egg cartons (non-Styrofoam)

## Avoid
- Recycling paper contaminated with grease or food (e.g., used pizza boxes, greasy napkins)
- Including wax-coated or plastic-laminated paper (juice boxes need special processing)
- Shredding paper into very fine pieces — short fibers are harder to recycle; cross-cut is okay
- Mixing in paper with metallic foil coatings (gift wrap that doesn't tear like paper)
- Throwing away paper that could be composted (napkins, tissues, paper towels → compost)

## Recommended Disposal
1. **Flatten cardboard boxes** to save space in recycling bins.
2. **Remove plastic tape and Styrofoam inserts** from shipping boxes before recycling.
3. **Compost** soiled paper towels, napkins, and non-glossy paper in a home or municipal compost bin.
4. **Shred sensitive documents** and recycle — most facilities accept cross-cut shreds in a paper bag.
5. **Reuse** boxes, wrapping paper, and envelopes when possible before recycling.

## Environmental Impact
- Recycling **one ton of paper** saves approximately **17 trees**, **26,500 liters of water**, and **2.5 m³ of landfill space**.
- Paper production accounts for roughly **4 %** of global energy use.
- Paper decomposes in landfills within **2–6 weeks**, but produces methane (a potent greenhouse gas) under anaerobic landfill conditions.
- The average office worker uses about **10,000 sheets of paper per year**.

## Eco Tip
Switch to digital statements, receipts, and note-taking wherever possible. When you must print, use both sides of the page and choose recycled-content paper — it requires **70 % less energy** to produce than virgin paper.
