# Plastic Waste

## Examples
- PET bottles (water, soda)
- HDPE containers (milk jugs, detergent bottles)
- Plastic bags and film wrap
- Polystyrene foam (Styrofoam cups, takeout containers)
- PVC pipes and packaging
- Plastic utensils and straws
- Food-grade cling wrap
- Blister packaging (pill packs, toy packaging)

## Avoid
- Mixing different resin types in the same recycling bin (check the number inside the ♻ symbol)
- Recycling plastic contaminated with food residue — rinse containers first
- Placing plastic bags in curbside single-stream recycling (they jam sorting machines)
- Assuming all plastics are recyclable — types 3 (PVC), 6 (PS), and 7 (Other) are rarely accepted
- Burning plastic at home — releases toxic dioxins and furans

## Recommended Disposal
1. **Check the resin code** (♻ 1–7) on the item and verify what your local facility accepts.
2. **Rinse and dry** food containers before placing them in recycling.
3. **Return plastic bags** to designated drop-off bins at grocery stores (do not place in curbside bins).
4. **Styrofoam**: Check for specialized drop-off programs; otherwise dispose in general waste.
5. **Hard-to-recycle plastics** (PVC, mixed-material packaging): Look for TerraCycle or manufacturer take-back programs.

## Environmental Impact
- Plastic can persist in the environment for **300–1,000+ years**.
- Approximately **8 million metric tons** of plastic enter the ocean every year.
- Microplastics have been found in drinking water, seafood, and human blood.
- Producing virgin plastic from fossil fuels emits roughly **6 kg of CO₂ per kg** of plastic manufactured.
- Only about **9 %** of all plastic ever produced has been recycled.

## Eco Tip
Carry a reusable water bottle and shopping bags to eliminate the most common single-use plastics from your daily routine. Small swaps compound — replacing just one disposable bottle a day keeps roughly **23 kg of plastic** out of landfills per year.
