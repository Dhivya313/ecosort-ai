# Metal Waste

## Examples
- Aluminum cans (soda, beer)
- Steel / tin cans (soup, vegetables, pet food)
- Aluminum foil and foil trays
- Metal bottle caps and jar lids
- Aerosol cans (empty)
- Scrap metal (hangers, pots, pans, tools)
- Copper wiring and plumbing fixtures
- Metal furniture frames

## Avoid
- Recycling aerosol cans that are not fully empty — residual pressure is a safety hazard
- Including metal contaminated with hazardous materials (paint cans with dried paint, chemical containers)
- Mixing in electronics with scrap metal — e-waste requires separate processing
- Placing small loose items (bottle caps, tabs) directly in the bin — group them inside a can and crimp the top shut so they aren't lost during sorting
- Throwing away large metal items in curbside bins — contact your local scrap yard or schedule a bulk pickup

## Recommended Disposal
1. **Rinse cans and tins** to remove food residue.
2. **Crush aluminum cans** to save bin space (check local guidance — some facilities prefer uncrushed).
3. **Clean aluminum foil** and ball it up to at least golf-ball size before recycling.
4. **Aerosol cans**: Ensure completely empty, do not puncture, and recycle with metals.
5. **Large scrap metal** (appliances, car parts): Take to a scrap metal yard — you may even earn money.

## Environmental Impact
- Recycling aluminum uses **95 % less energy** than producing it from raw bauxite ore.
- A recycled aluminum can returns to the shelf as a new can in as little as **60 days**.
- Steel is the **most recycled material on Earth** — over **80 %** of steel is eventually recycled.
- Mining virgin metal ore causes significant habitat destruction, water pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions.

## Eco Tip
Keep a dedicated container in your kitchen for rinsed cans and metal lids — it makes sorting effortless on collection day. Aluminum is one of the highest-value recyclables, so recycling it consistently has both environmental and economic benefits.
