# Organic Waste

## Examples
- Fruit and vegetable scraps (peels, cores, rinds)
- Coffee grounds and tea bags (remove staples)
- Eggshells
- Bread, pasta, rice, and cereal
- Yard waste (grass clippings, leaves, small branches)
- Houseplant trimmings
- Nutshells (except walnut — can inhibit plant growth)
- Paper towels and napkins (unbleached, non-greasy preferred for compost)

## Avoid
- Composting meat, fish, and dairy at home without a specialized system — they attract pests and create odors (municipal/industrial composting can handle these)
- Adding diseased plants or invasive weeds to a home compost pile — seeds and pathogens may survive
- Including pet waste (dog, cat) in garden compost — it can contain harmful parasites
- Placing "compostable" plastic items in a home bin — most require industrial composting temperatures (55–60 °C)
- Over-loading compost with only one type of input — maintain a balance of greens (nitrogen) and browns (carbon)

## Recommended Disposal
1. **Home composting**: Layer greens (food scraps, grass) with browns (dry leaves, cardboard) in a 1:3 ratio. Turn regularly and keep moist.
2. **Municipal green bin**: Use your city's organic waste collection for items that cannot be home-composted (meat, dairy, bones).
3. **Vermicomposting**: Use a worm bin for small-space indoor composting of fruit and vegetable scraps.
4. **Yard waste**: Mulch or take to a community drop-off site.
5. **Cooking oil**: Small amounts can be absorbed with paper towels and composted; large quantities should go to a used-oil collection point.

## Environmental Impact
- Organic waste in landfills decomposes anaerobically and produces **methane**, which is **80× more potent** than CO₂ as a greenhouse gas over 20 years.
- Roughly **30–40 %** of all food produced globally is wasted.
- Composting returns valuable nutrients to the soil, reducing the need for synthetic fertilizers.
- Diverting organics from landfills can cut a household's waste volume by up to **50 %**.

## Eco Tip
Start a small countertop compost caddy to collect scraps while cooking. Even if you don't have a garden, many communities offer free compost drop-off points. Composting is the single most impactful step most households can take to reduce landfill-bound waste.
