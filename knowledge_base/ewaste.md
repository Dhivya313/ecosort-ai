# Electronic Waste (E-Waste)

## Examples
- Smartphones, tablets, and laptops
- Desktop computers and monitors
- Keyboards, mice, and webcams
- Televisions (CRT, LCD, LED)
- Printers and scanners
- Batteries (lithium-ion, alkaline, button cells)
- Cables, chargers, and adapters
- Small appliances (toasters, hair dryers, electric razors)
- Circuit boards and hard drives
- LED and CFL light bulbs

## Avoid
- Throwing e-waste in general household trash — it contains hazardous materials (lead, mercury, cadmium, flame retardants)
- Attempting to incinerate or crush batteries — lithium-ion batteries can ignite or explode
- Dumping e-waste in landfills — toxic chemicals leach into soil and groundwater
- Hoarding old electronics indefinitely — battery degradation over time increases fire risk
- Using unregulated or informal e-waste recyclers — improper processing exposes workers to toxins and pollutes surrounding areas

## Recommended Disposal
1. **Manufacturer take-back programs**: Many brands (Apple, Dell, Samsung, HP) offer free mail-in or in-store recycling.
2. **Certified e-waste recyclers**: Look for **e-Stewards** or **R2-certified** facilities to ensure responsible processing.
3. **Retail drop-off**: Stores like Best Buy, Staples, and some mobile carriers accept old electronics.
4. **Batteries**: Use dedicated battery recycling bins at retail stores or community collection events — never place in curbside recycling.
5. **Data security**: Factory-reset devices and remove SIM/SD cards before recycling.
6. **Donate or resell**: If the device still works, extend its life through donation or resale platforms.

## Environmental Impact
- The world generates roughly **62 million metric tons** of e-waste per year, and less than **23 %** is formally recycled.
- A single smartphone contains over **30 different elements**, including rare earth metals whose mining causes severe ecological damage.
- Improper e-waste disposal releases **lead, mercury, and cadmium** — all neurotoxic heavy metals — into the environment.
- Recycling one million cell phones recovers approximately **16,000 kg of copper**, **350 kg of silver**, **34 kg of gold**, and **15 kg of palladium**.

## Eco Tip
Before upgrading, ask yourself: *does my current device truly need replacing?* Extending the life of a smartphone by even one year significantly reduces its lifetime carbon footprint. When you do upgrade, trade in or donate your old device — and always use a certified recycler for anything that can't be reused.
